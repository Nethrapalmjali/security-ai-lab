How it works

  • Caesar: only 26 keys. Each is scored with a chi-squared distance from English letter frequencies.
  • Vigenère: the index of coincidence (≈0.067 English, ≈0.038 random) of every n-th letter reveals the key length; each column is then a Caesar cipher. Kasiski examination of repeated trigrams gives a second opinion.
  • XOR: single-byte keys are brute-forced and scored; repeating keys use normalised Hamming distance to find the key size.

Short texts (under ~100 letters) give unreliable statistics — that is a real limitation of the attack.
